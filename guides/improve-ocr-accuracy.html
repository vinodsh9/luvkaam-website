How to improve OCR accuracy

Updated 23 October 2026

When text recognition goes wrong, the cause is usually the picture, not the tool. A few small changes before you upload can turn a messy result into a clean one.

1. Use a sharp, high-resolution image

Letters need enough pixels to be told apart. For a scanned page, 300 dots per inch is a good target. For a phone photo, hold the phone steady and let the camera focus before you shoot.

2. Shoot straight on

Angled photos stretch letters at one end of the line. Place the page flat and hold the phone parallel to it.

3. Light the page evenly

Shadows and glare break letters into pieces. Use daylight or a lamp from the side, and avoid flash directly over glossy paper.

4. Crop to the text

Remove borders, desk edges and photos that are not part of the text. Less clutter means fewer false characters.

5. Keep strong contrast

Dark text on a plain light background reads best. If the page is faded, raise the contrast in your phone's photo editor before you use it.

6. Choose the right file type

Screenshots and scans of text usually look cleaner as PNG. Heavy JPG compression can blur edges. Our image format guide explains the difference.

7. Know the limits

Handwriting, stylised fonts, very small print and text over busy pictures often fail whatever you do. Match the tool to the language too. Luvkaam's Image to Text supports English at the moment.

Always proofread. Even a perfect-looking result can swap characters such as 0 and O or 1 and l. Check names, dates and numbers against the original.
